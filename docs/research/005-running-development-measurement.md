Date: 2026-11-24 | Revision: 0.1 | Status: **Draft — pending independent human review**

## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 Findings and decisions

- **Running dose is multidimensional.** More running and longer habitual runs are associated with faster half-marathon performance, but observational associations do not establish the benefit of adding mileage to an individual athlete's current program. There is no validated universal progression rate, optimal frequency or individual diminishing-returns curve. A productive dose can be held (RT-P02, CT-P05). Changes in weekly distance and the size of individual runs require separate review.
- **There is actual adaptive-program evidence.** A small controlled trial in recreational runners (Nuuttila 2022) found greater 10-km improvement from a multivariable, twice-weekly adjustment strategy than predefined training. This is important positive evidence, not proof that increasing mileage on favorable feedback is optimal, that its exact decision thresholds generalize, or that overnight wearable metrics alone determine a prescription. HRV-guided interventions more narrowly address timing of harder sessions and have uncertain pooled performance benefits.
- **Do not encode the 10% rule.** A novice randomized program comparison did not demonstrate prevention of injury. A much larger 2025 observational study found associations with increases in single-session distance relative to the preceding longest run, not week-to-week mileage. These are different exposures and designs. Neither proves a safe boundary or an individual injury predictor.
- **No universal 80/20.** Polarized training has a small pooled VO2peak advantage over heterogeneous alternatives in one review, but not a clear time-trial advantage; individual-participant evidence does not establish polarized superiority over pyramidal training. Session-goal, time-in-zone and distance-in-zone distributions are different estimands. Preserve the model and denominator.
- **Select running sessions by intended adaptation and cost.** Low-intensity running, sustained harder running and intervals can coexist. High-intensity intervals can improve VO2max and running performance, but the tested protocols do not prescribe a universal weekly dose. Long runs have race-specific and potential durability value; their incremental benefit in a hybrid athlete is uncertain. Heavy lower-body lifting and demanding running compete for scheduling and fueling resources (Packets 003–004).
- **Intensity anchors are estimates with different meanings.** Percent maximum HR, HR reserve, threshold HR, pace, critical speed, recent performances and perceived effort are not interchangeable. Use a declared method and uncertainty; do not reconstruct historical physiological zones from today's device configuration. An automatically revised threshold is an estimate change, not proof of adaptation.
- **Observe performance, not imaginary physiology.** A standardized time trial measures task performance. Faster pace at comparable effort/HR under matched conditions is evidence consistent with improved field performance, not a direct measurement of laboratory economy or VO2max. Wearable VO2max agreement with laboratory values can be poor despite small mean bias. Agreement across individuals does not establish longitudinal responsiveness.
- **Durability is interesting but not yet a reliable adaptive trigger.** Prolonged-running laboratory studies show that repeatability differs markedly by outcome and by whether absolute fatigued values or changes are measured. Split-based HR/speed decoupling is a field descriptor only; it is confounded and needs paired segment data. No evidence identified supports a universal decoupling threshold for progression or readiness.
- **Keep provider integration separate.** Canonical observations and decision-specific data quality determine practical applicability. Training guidance may be possible without HR, nutrition logs, threshold tests or high-resolution files. Missing essential data constrain specificity; missing optional data do not automatically block a recommendation. Scientific readiness, practical data readiness and authorization are separate.

Target: adult recreational athletes experienced in both running and lifting, aiming at general aerobic fitness or 5 km, 10 km, half-marathon development, possibly maintaining a secondary strength or hypertrophy goal. Primary outcomes: standardized running performance and preservation of specified strength outcomes; secondary: training exposure, intensity, fatigue, adherence, tolerance, aerobic physiology and health. **Volume and frequency synthesis.** Distance, duration, frequency, session concentration and intensity are not equivalent. E01–E02 describe associations conditional on measured covariates; neither identifies a dose-response curve or diminishing returns. No adequately appraised matched-weekly-dose frequency experiment in experienced hybrid runners was found in this focused screen. Distributing an equivalent duration among more sessions changes per-session load and recovery opportunities; fewer sessions may be more feasible or provide a specific continuous-duration stimulus. These are planning tradeoffs, not proven rankings. Additional training consumes time, carbohydrate and recovery resources even when marginal benefit is uncertain. Use multiple baseline-relative exposure dimensions: completed running time/distance, count, longest comparable run, purpose/intensity, recent interruptions and trends. Report actual coverage and distinguish habitual from prescribed. Do not calculate an increase from a missing or zero denominator as if well-defined. E07 supports testing adaptive decisions as a class; the separate effects of changed dose, timing, monitoring and adherence remain unidentified. No retrieved trial isolates a feedback-driven mileage-only policy against a cumulative-dose-matched fixed policy in dual-experienced athletes.

**Definitions and reconciliation.** Three-zone physiological model: zone 1 below first turnpoint, zone 2 between first and second boundaries, zone 3 above second. The exact laboratory methods (VT1/GET, LT1, RCP, LT2, CS) are not perfectly equivalent; the heavy/severe boundary deserves special care (E20). Pyramidal: low > middle > high; polarized: low predominates and high exceeds middle; threshold-focused: greater middle-intensity emphasis, definition study-specific. Predominantly low-intensity is not necessarily polarized unless high and middle allocation is known. HIIT describes a session method, not an entire distribution. Five/seven-zone models subdivide differently; the same numeral can denote different domains. Never map zone 2 across systems by label. Keep boundary provenance, effective date, activity mode, denominator and unknown time. Session-goal classification counts a hard interval day as hard; time-in-zone includes warmups, recoveries and HR lag; distance weights faster work more than time. For an identical run, 10 min warmup + 12 min hard work + 8 min easy recovery/cooldown = 12/30 min intended hard work, but one hard session by session goal (illustrative arithmetic, not a target). Achieved physiological time may differ from intended time. Report both when supportable, never substitute one for another. E11 and E13 are not independent evidence; assess shared studies, aggregate vs individual models and different comparators. E15 tests sequence but not hybrid periodization. Low-intensity predominance with purposeful harder work is a reasonable candidate approach, not a mandatory ratio; availability and concurrent costs can alter the distribution.

**Practical intensity framework (methodology):** distinguish moderate-domain stable responses below the first threshold, heavy-domain between first threshold and critical speed (or another explicitly defined upper anchor), and severe-domain above critical speed where a sustainable steady state is not expected. Do not use a commercial zone label as the physiological definition. An HR-based anchor may lag at onset of intervals or change with heat/fatigue; pace is an external speed, not metabolic demand. HR reserve = HRmax − resting HR, and a fraction f of reserve is resting HR + f×reserve; this arithmetic does not validate the chosen f. Maximum HR percentage = HR/HRmax; threshold HR percentage = HR/estimated threshold HR. Do not crosswalk by matching percentages. Keep the measured or estimated anchor, sport, protocol, units, date, quality and uncertainty. Age-prediction is coarse population context only; actual high-quality maximal effort observations can inform a review, but a high observed value from a corrupted sensor cannot. Distinguish a device threshold update from actual physiological change. Reassess when the anchor is contradicted by credible comparable performances, when the method/sensor changes, after meaningful changes in training or health, or when a planned assessment is appropriate; no calendar schedule is established by these studies. A field assessment is optional and must fit fatigue, safety and goals. For sustained steady work, combine pace, HR and perceived effort where available; for short reps, use planned work duration, credible pace/effort and recovery rather than accelerating to reach HR immediately. For easy running, conversation and comfortable effort can guide a qualitative prescription without HR. A recent race result supports race-specific pace context but does not uniquely identify a threshold, economy or VO2max. Critical speed is a model-dependent distance-time relationship: fit only suitable maximal performances across different durations with quality controls and estimate uncertainty; a single ordinary run cannot identify both CS and D′. E23 is one tested protocol, not a default assessment.

Do not conflate agreement, reliability and sensitivity. A small group mean bias can conceal large individual errors. A large ICC can reflect wide between-athlete spread rather than precise within-athlete change. A correlation is not agreement; a typical error is not the smallest worthwhile change. If a repeatability study establishes an appropriate within-person standard error (SEM) under the same conditions, an illustrative statistical convention for the 95% minimum detectable difference between two independent readings is 1.96×sqrt(2)×SEM, assuming independent Gaussian errors and no systematic shift. This is a conditional statistical derivation, not an athlete threshold. A practically meaningful performance change is a separate decision requiring athlete goal, uncertainty and costs. None of the reviewed estimates justifies a universal change cutoff for this product.

Candidate longitudinal approach: compare standardized tasks (same distance/course or known treadmill setting, duration, warmup, effort intent, sensor method and comparable environmental context). Preserve raw results and protocol versions. Aggregate repeated comparable observations with uncertainty rather than ranking all runs by a single best pace/HR ratio. Flag conditions and deviations before aggregation; use repeated matched observations or a pre-specified robust summary when enough data exist; defer detailed state estimation and individualized error calibration to Packet 009. A single valid race can establish that the athlete ran a faster race under those conditions; it cannot establish why. Do not suppress genuine performance because a wearable score stayed unchanged. A faster run with more effort is not evidence of improved efficiency. Neither a flat training trend nor missing maximal testing establishes a physiological plateau. Ask about heat, terrain, fatigue, footwear, stops, sensor changes, recent strength training and fueling when those explanations could change the decision. Avoid mandatory feedback on every easy run.

**Operationally meaningful granularity (candidate measurement convention, not validated diagnostic):** If early and late segments have paired mean HR and speed and comparable conditions, define internal/external ratio q=HR/speed (e.g. bpm per m/s). Relative change = 100×(q_late/q_early−1). This definition is dimensionless after ratio, positive when HR cost per unit speed rises. It corresponds algebraically to relative change in Smyth's normalized ratio within a run if reference HRmax and CS are fixed, but different segmentation, pace conventions, moving time and conditions change the metric. Other conventions use speed/HR and reverse percentage direction; retain formula version and denominator. Illustrative arithmetic only: if paired ratios were 40 and 44, relative change is +10%; not a fitness cutoff. Exclude warm-up/transients, stops, interval recovery, invalid HR, inconsistent grade and non-comparable segments or label confounding; require ordered timestamps/segment durations, distances, paired HR, time coverage and sensor quality. Use time-weighted HR and total distance/elapsed or moving time consistently for segment speed; do not average pace or ratios across unequal segments indiscriminately. A whole-run average HR and average pace cannot reconstruct early/late values. Even valid split summaries may be too coarse if effort changes within the segments; fine aligned time-series, elevation, temperature, cadence and fueling can improve appraisal but not eliminate physiological ambiguity. Do not assume an increase in HR/speed is a direct measure of rising oxygen cost, dehydration or glycogen depletion. Laboratory durability may reflect distinct changes in economy, thresholds, peak speed or capacity under fatigue; evidence for independence of adaptations or a dose-response to long runs is incomplete. No field-derived decoupling threshold is validated here for assigning intensity zones, diagnosing fitness, escalating mileage or canceling lifting. Use the descriptor for exploratory repeated comparison or feedback only.

Maintain workload as a vector, not a scalar optimization target. External exposure: distance (km), duration (min), frequency, longest run, grade/surface if known, high-intensity work duration and event-specific exposure. Internal response: HR (bpm or relative to a declared valid anchor), session RPE on its declared scale, and optional sRPE×duration (arbitrary units). Escalating pain, persistent unexplained symptoms or concerning nutrition/health patterns require appropriate professional review, not a numerical injury-risk prediction.

## 3. Candidate coaching policies

All policies below are revision 0.1, pending independent human review. They are provider-independent candidate methodologies, not adopted planner rules. Evidence IDs refer to section 2. The observation categories are F = feasible, C = conditionally feasible, Q = qualitative only, D = deferred. Classifications indicate practical data supportability in the working MVP context, conditional on verified coverage, and do not imply scientific approval. Prescription (P), adherence (A) and effectiveness (E) are evaluated separately. In every policy, current restrictions, symptoms, athlete priorities, available time and approval remain binding. A concerning symptom, suspected injury or nutrition/health risk can require professional assessment and a safe modification or deferral rather than maintaining the plan blindly. No universally safe dose, spacing, numerical response threshold or training-readiness cutoff is introduced.

**Adaptive trial detail for reviewers (E07, Fig. 1):** Nuuttila et al. evaluated training on Mondays and Thursdays. During the volume phase an increase was an addition of 5 percentage points of baseline volume (e.g. 1.10 to 1.15 times baseline); a decrease reduced current volume by 25%. In the interval phase an increase could add a HIT session; reduction also removed HIT. The number of HIT sessions was capped by the experimental block (two or three); post-recovery progression depended on indicators. These are the study's rules, not proposed numerical thresholds. ## 4. Periodization methodology

### 4.1 Evidence-supported principles versus candidate organization

E07 provides a directly tested endurance-only longitudinal structure (preparation, volume, interval phases) with adaptive rather than exclusively predetermined adjustments. E15 tests distribution sequencing in trained runners; E11–16 do not establish a universal sequence or optimum distribution. E18 supports interval work for aerobic capacity under a specific matched-oxygen-consumption protocol. E01–02 and E31 provide associations, not evidence for compulsory volume or long-run escalation. Packet 003 supports considering both strength and endurance effects; Packet 004 supports considering energy and session fuel. No retrieved experiment tests the three proposed hybrid block archetypes with joint adaptation, equal realized workload, nutritional control and both primary and secondary outcomes. Exact phase duration, minimum maintenance dose, progression increments and taper timing remain unapproved.

### 4.2 Three archetypes (candidate methods; not fixed calendars)

| Block | Running intent | Strength and nutrition coordination | Adaptation and assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength emphasis | Preserve agreed aerobic/running outcomes; maintain familiar easy exposure and selected harder work if needed/tolerated, avoid increasing for its own sake | Protect priority lifts, especially lower-body; don't assume all running interferes; retain fuel for demanding sessions and energy adequacy | Compare secondary running outcome with baseline; if deteriorating, review exposure, fatigue/fuel and goal, not simply add long run. |
| Endurance emphasis | Develop event-specific running exposure and quality; progress either volume, sustained/interval work or long-run specificity when justified | Maintain agreed priority lifts with suitable intensity/exposure; redistribute lower-body lifting if conflict; fuel longer and harder running and close sessions | Compare target running task and secondary strength, adherence and fatigue; if secondary performance decline credible, review tradeoff explicitly. |
| Balanced development | Select complementary running sessions and moderate progression/hold opportunities; avoid assuming both disciplines can always progress at once | Preserve both primary session qualities; time budget and fueling feasibility may constrain both | Compare both outcomes and costs; choose hold or redistribute if currently productive; discuss priority changes if infeasible. |

### 4.3 Phase-specific choices (not experimentally optimized hybrid sequence)

| Phase | Proposed running focus | What changes / what must be protected |
|---|---|---|
| Aerobic base | Familiar comfortable running, consistency, tolerable exposure; optional strides or controlled quality | Establish baseline of actual dose and comparable field outcome; hold if productive. No obligatory low-intensity-only phase. |
| Higher-volume accumulation | If goal warrants, distribute duration/frequency or extend long run relative to actual tolerance | Avoid concentrating unfamiliar distance; protect lifting quality and energy availability; no fixed weekly rate. |
| Threshold / high-intensity development | Select controlled sustained work or intervals for goal; keep easy exposure feasible | Do not add hard sessions merely to satisfy ratio; inspect actual quality, effort, subsequent lifts and fuel. |
| Race specificity | Practice event-specific pacing, duration and fueling; preserve priority strength constraints | Use credible current race context, not aspirational target as physiological anchor; taper conflicts reviewed. |
| Assessment | Standardized TT/race or submaximal task; choose endpoint matching goal | Separate actual performance from estimated physiology; account for preparation, conditions, fatigue and secondary outcomes. |
| Taper / recovery / interruption | Reduce unnecessary fatigue, maintain selected specificity when appropriate; resume with reassessed baseline | No forced catch-up; do not equate taper with total cessation. Detailed coordinated taper and maintenance reserved for 006. |

### 4.4 Objective-specific illustrative choices

- General aerobic fitness: repeatable enjoyable exposure and submaximal performance context; a race or maximal test is optional. Avoid treating wearable VO2max as required.
- Faster 5 km: relatively greater relevance of high-quality faster running and direct 5 km performance; a long run may support base but no fixed duration or target distribution.
- Faster 10 km: mix sustained/threshold-oriented work and appropriate intervals with sufficient familiar exposure; assess directly at 10 km or a documented alternative.
- Half marathon: continuous-duration tolerance and specific pace/fueling become more salient; longer runs may be considered relative to history, not required beyond event distance; assess late-session tolerance and actual race performance.
- Endurance maintenance during strength specialization: agree what maintenance means (e.g., comparable field performance or event ability, not just number of runs); preserve adequate exposure without assuming two easy runs maintain every quality. See Packet 003 and pending maintenance work in 006.

All examples are programming judgment. The planner should record the objective, selected session purposes, actual exposure and comparable assessments, and the reason to choose a progression dimension. A later week may differ without invalidating the block; an objective or secondary-performance commitment change needs explicit block review. No numeric intensification/deload/taper schedule, universal spacing or hidden optimization of one discipline is authorized.

## 5. Draft J1 evidence catalog additions

### 5.1 Staging and representation rules

Use the exact existing J1 schema (`SourceRevision`, `ClaimRevision`, `Question`, `Relation`, `MethodRevision`) as a fragment, not a release. Preserve all original records and review statuses. The fragment below deliberately selects narrower, extractable results. Other entries are contextual or screening leads, not absent findings. Measurement and association claims are scoped to the measured comparison, not represented as treatment effects. J1 can store descriptive assertion, result and restriction; its existing scope fields do not encode typed estimands, reliability protocol, agreement-vs-repeatability or study design in a machine-readable way on the claim itself. Future reviewed representation may require distinct question types and structured measurement statistics with reference methods, units, bias, LoA, ICC, within-person error and uncertainty. Do not use these draft measurement or association claims as intervention efficacy. Consensus, explanatory mechanisms and exact decision parameters remain in research prose or candidate methods, not randomized-effect claims. No schema change is proposed here.

Publication dates are known dates from accessed bibliographic records or publisher dates, not fabricated day-of-month from issue year. Publication-status/rights checks remain preliminary; only the INTERLIVE correction was specifically inspected in accessible text. For each record, `checked_on` means this session's targeted bibliographic/result check, not a comprehensive Crossmark/retraction search. `next_review` is a proposed review date (not approval). Where access was limited to indexed original passages, the fragment conservatively uses `abstract` and explains the additional material in appraisal; verify full-text status before promotion. All sources and claims in this fragment have `status: pending`, `approval: null`; no approved transfer arguments. Quality labels are provisional for the narrow result. Use existing prior-packet sources for E39–41 rather than duplicating publications. Relations link only same-question claims and are omitted if a defensible edge is not yet appraised. Methods are candidates and may reference several questions; eligibility requires independent review and release approval.

### 6.1 Evaluation beyond synthetic conformance

Score unsupported empirical statements, source applicability, missingness disclosures, observation eligibility, feasible alternatives, approval authority and hard restrictions separately. Use paired perturbations (e.g., matched route versus heat; missing versus actually low HR; identical canonical observations with different provider provenance; pending versus reviewed support). Pin interpretation methods, units, time windows, zone definitions, comparison conditions and exact evidence versions. Check whether the same input produces the same semantic recommendation. Test prescription, adherence assessment and effectiveness reporting independently.

For later prospective evaluation (Packet 009), compare a fixed goal-specific running program, a simple baseline-relative progression/hold policy, and a reviewed joint adaptive policy. Predefine outcome and follow-up, such as standardized event-specific performance, priority-lift retention, adherence/coverage, time burden, athlete-reported fatigue/tolerance and adverse events. Track actual rather than just prescribed exposure, missing and partial sessions, changing conditions, nutrition and concurrent lifting. Avoid claiming efficacy from retrospective correlations or synthetic pass rates. A prospective design should assess whether extra observations add decision value over simple effort and performance history, and whether collection burden worsens adherence. Specify analysis, comparison, safety monitoring and uncertainty before data collection; no sample-size or progression cutoff is asserted here.
