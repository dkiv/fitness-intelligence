## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 Findings and implications

Fitness Intelligence's target is adults with meaningful experience in both running and resistance training. Nutrition is a resource constraint and an intervention, not a separate weight-management objective. An increase in running load changes the nutritional context of the next strength session; the reverse also holds. No retrieved study validates a complete adaptive hybrid nutrition policy.

- **Energy availability (EA) differs from energy balance.** Stable body mass, strength gains, a wearable's calorie total or a seemingly adequate daily food log do not exclude problematic low EA. Clinical risk assessment and ordinary training decisions are different tasks.
- **Energy restriction can compromise muscular and endurance outcomes.** A controlled combined-training study in trained women found lower integrated muscle protein synthesis during low EA despite provided protein. A separate controlled endurance study found impaired time-trial performance. The resistance-training meta-analysis suggests impaired lean-mass gains, with uncertain strength effects. None supplies an individualized safe deficit or diagnostic threshold.
- **Fuel the work required.** Carbohydrate ingestion improves performance in many prolonged endurance protocols and can improve resistance-training volume in demanding acute sessions. Prior fueling, intensity, muscle groups, session duration and proximity of the next session matter. Short, fed easy runs and heavy low-repetition lifting are not interchangeable with prolonged or glycogen-depleting work.
- **Acute and chronic results are not interchangeable.** Better glycogen restoration, repetitions or muscle protein synthesis do not establish greater multiweek performance gains. Periodized carbohydrate restriction has not shown a clear pooled performance advantage over normal/high availability in trained endurance athletes. Do not automatically prescribe train-low sessions.
- **Protein supports both disciplines, but a hybrid-specific requirement is unresolved.** Direct concurrent supplementation studies show some muscular benefit; evidence for extra aerobic-capacity benefit is weak. Resistance-training and endurance metabolic evidence provide complementary, indirect context. Total intake is a more defensible first priority than minute-perfect meal timing.
- **Nutrition targets must be jointly feasible.** Preserve energy adequacy, protein, carbohydrate availability for priority work and fat/essential nutrient adequacy. Do not independently maximize macronutrients or use fixed percentages without regard to energy and workload. No default surplus, deficit or calorie target is justified for every athlete.
- **Missingness is a first-class state.** Nutrition advice can exist without food logs, but adequacy inference requires coverage and provenance. Daily totals cannot establish meal timing. Even meal logs do not measure glycogen or physiological EA. The coach must distinguish a reported low-intake day from missing or partially imported data.
- **Human safety and agency supersede optimization.** Concerning symptoms, disordered eating concerns or suspected bone stress injury warrant professional evaluation, not an automated cut, a REDs label or numerical risk score. Preserve prior approval and block objectives when proposing a change; do not silently rewrite training.

## 2. Scientific evidence table

### 2.1 NU-01 — energy availability, energy balance and adaptation

Definitions: energy balance = energy intake (EI) minus total energy expenditure (TEE); persistent imbalance affects stored energy. EA = (EI − exercise energy expenditure above non-exercise expenditure during the bout) / fat-free mass (FFM), conventionally kcal/kg FFM/day. EA is not the subtraction of wearable total calories, and active calories are not automatically exercise expenditure. The concept describes energy left for physiological functions, not a diagnosis. Expenditure and physiological function can adapt downward, so energy balance at a reduced expenditure can coexist with problematic LEA; short-term water shifts can also mask tissue changes. Stable weight is therefore not reassuring on its own. Conversely, weight loss does not prove REDs. See IOC definitions and clinical assessment sections (A01).

### 2.2 NU-02 — carbohydrate availability and session-specific fueling

Muscle glycogen is local to recruited muscle; liver glycogen and circulating glucose also support endurance work. Hard running and repeated high-volume sets can draw substantially on carbohydrate. A single easy run or low-volume heavy lift is less informative about depletion than workload, prior sessions, dietary intake and individual response together. Low carbohydrate availability is distinct from low total energy availability, although they can coincide. Neither a daily carbohydrate log nor heart rate is a direct glycogen measurement. Mechanistic context: Thomas et al., carbohydrate section, and Burke et al. 2017 below.

#### Conditional consensus reference ranges (not policy defaults)

Source B09, Academy version [original full text](https://www.andeal.org/files/files/Collaborations/Position_Athletic-Performance_2016March.pdf), table pp. 508–509, protein needs and fat sections. The carbohydrate values are intended for high availability where performance or quality is important; they are not minimum requirements for each calendar day. Values describe intake per kg **body mass** except during-event grams per hour. These reference values are not estimates of EA or energy expenditure and must not be summed mechanically. Check total energy feasibility, tolerance, dietary restrictions and context before recommending numbers; personalized plans require appropriately qualified review.

| Context in source | Reference value | Transfer/decision caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Light/skill or low-intensity work | 3–5 g CHO/kg/day | Not a compulsory rest-day minimum. |
| Moderate exercise ~1 h/day | 5–7 g/kg/day | Not derived from one hour of lifting plus one easy run. |
| Endurance ~1–3 h/day moderate-to-hard | 6–10 g/kg/day | Assess cumulative demand; durations are descriptive categories. |
| Extreme moderate-to-hard volume >4–5 h/day | 8–12 g/kg/day | Rare for target population; specialist support. |
| Before prolonged exercise (>60 min) | 1–4 g/kg, 1–4 h before | Timing/portion adjusted for gut comfort and prior meal. |
| During brief exercise (<45 min) | No routine CHO needed | Conditional on adequate prior fueling; not advice to fast. |
| Sustained hard work 45–75 min | Small amounts, including mouth rinse | Not mandatory for intervals; mouth rinse is not energy intake. |
| Endurance ~1–2.5 h | 30–60 g/h | Practice tolerated amounts; duration alone does not determine dose. |
| Very prolonged >2.5–3 h | Up to 90 g/h | Multiple transportable carbohydrates at higher intakes, gut training; emerging higher-dose evidence not definitive (B10). |
| Two fuel-demanding sessions with <8 h recovery | 1–1.2 g/kg/h in first 4 h, then normal daily needs | A rapid-refueling scenario, not a universal spacing or intake rule for double sessions. |
| Carbohydrate loading for >90 min sustained event | 10–12 g/kg/day for 36–48 h | Event-specific, not every race or strength assessment; practice and review tolerance. |

**Session interpretation:** Heavy low-rep resistance sessions rely on high-rate energy provision but do not necessarily benefit from a drink when adequately fed; high-volume sets or a prior hard run make CHO planning more salient (B01/B02). Easy short running usually needs no special during-session intake if adequately fueled; long runs and sustained threshold or VO₂max intervals warrant attention to prior meals and duration, not automatic supplementation. Race preparation can use rehearsed intake and GI tolerance. For double days ask whether the first session plausibly depleted muscle groups relevant to the next priority session, how much recovery time is actually available, and whether meals can fit. No trial here identifies optimal timing independently of total daily energy and CHO for an experienced hybrid athlete. Replacing food with a sports drink or adding calories are different interventions; avoid claiming CHO-specific causality from water controls. Adequate daily energy does not guarantee appropriate pre-session timing, and high acute availability need not maximize all signaling adaptations. The evidence for train-low performance benefit is insufficient to routinely sacrifice quality or risk inadequate intake.

| C12 / R | Schoenfeld, Aragon & Krieger 2013 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-10-53), abstract Results/full meta-regression. **Synthesis and denominator selection.** Total daily protein is a useful first-level nutritional variable; it is neither a recovery score nor an energy adequacy proxy. Consensus B09 uses 1.2–2.0 g/kg **body mass** per day for athletes and discusses higher amounts during restriction/intensified training. An approximately 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day discussion range for some adequately fueled hybrid adults is an overlap of resistance and endurance literature, not a validated hybrid dose or operational default. The upper confidence bound in C06 and C08's estimates may inform a discussion of uncertainty, not automatic escalation. The studies in restriction (C09/C10) tested different large contrasts; they do not establish an optimal intake for lean, experienced hybrid athletes on moderate deficits. Avoid adding the endurance and strength requirements together. Prefer ordinary protein-containing meals spaced across the day, considering dietary preferences, quality and feasibility. B09's examples of ~0.25–0.3 g/kg per eating opportunity near demanding exercise and periodically through the day are consensus references; newer endurance metabolic proposals are not established chronic timing targets. A body-mass denominator enables comparison with most reviewed evidence; FFM is a different quantity and may be relevant in some restriction studies or where body mass is not representative. Do not convert without reliable FFM and matching source units. Never infer lean mass from a consumer scale as precise tissue mass. Medical conditions affecting protein advice require qualified individualized guidance. Protein supplements are optional food substitutes, not prescribed treatment.

A fixed 40/30/30 split or any other fixed percentage has no demonstrated joint optimum. At a given energy intake, adding protein or fat can displace carbohydrate; high CHO targets alongside protein and fat targets can be infeasible. Review dietary pattern, available meals, adequate energy, carbohydrate opportunities, essential fats and micronutrient variety together. Do not force the macros to fit a calorie ceiling that makes the combined goals infeasible. This packet excludes comprehensive micronutrient/supplement review; iron deficiency and other medical/nutrient issues are differential explanations, not diagnoses by the coach.

### 2.5 NU-05 — evidence for longitudinal nutritional periodization

B07 compares restricted versus normal/high carbohydrate strategies, not a dynamic intake-by-week system. B08 includes high-CHO and periodized-high-CHO arms with similar total macronutrients, providing a stronger timing-context comparison than many non-isocaloric studies; both improved race performance, but a relative benefit of one high-CHO pattern over the other is not established by their within-group intervals. B05 concerns timing during short recovery and B01 immediate lifting volume. C11 demonstrates a timing effect on an acute biomarker at matched recovery protein; C12 tempers long-term interpretation. A02/A03 are short controlled exposures, not trials of athlete-approved longitudinal adaptive coaching. There is no demonstrated optimal daily versus weekly optimization horizon here. Treat session timing, daily adequacy and multiweek body-composition goals as distinct but coupled layers; see §4.

**Observation semantics.** Total EE normally comprises resting/basal energy expenditure, thermic effect of food and physical activity (including exercise and nonexercise activity). BMR is measured under stricter conditions than RMR; neither is directly measured by most watches. Provider basal/active/total fields may use proprietary conventions; verify units, day boundary and overlap. Do not sum total plus active or exercise calories or count an imported Garmin activity twice via Health Connect. Garmin Personal API availability and sampled nutrition/weight fields are project-provided context, not independently verified comprehensive coverage. Preserve source attribution, ingest timestamps, timezone, completeness status, revisions and whether a value is measured, self-reported, estimated or unknown. Distinguish daily nutrition entries from daily summaries and mirrored imports. Meal timing requires actual timestamp/date and attribution; absence of a meal event does not mean fasting.

A longer-run body-mass trend under comparable measurement conditions can constrain hypotheses about energy balance. Acute changes may reflect hydration, sodium, glycogen-associated water, gut contents, menstrual-cycle effects or training inflammation. A taper or carbohydrate loading can increase mass without equivalent tissue gain. A stable scale cannot validate the calorie log or exclude LEA; a declining trend cannot identify a specific deficit or diagnosis. Intake and expenditure both noisy: if intake is underreported, a model fitting weight change can be systematically wrong about TEE. Do not correct individual logs by 19%, apply a fixed watch discount, equate activity calories with EEE, or infer kcal/day from a few weigh-ins using a constant energy-per-kg conversion. Packet 007 owns identifiability, statistical modeling, validation and uncertainty calibration; no estimator is specified here.

### 2.7 Scientific synthesis, evidence hierarchy and unresolved causal questions

| Evidence relationship | What is supported | What is not supported |
|---|---|---|
| Direct combined exercise A02, C01–C04, C09 | Specific physiological/body-composition responses under concurrent exercise conditions | An optimal macro prescription or proof of benefits in dual-experienced recreational runners; C04 cannot isolate protein |
| Single-discipline A03, A05–A08, B01–B08, C05–C12 | Specific intervention effects or metabolic associations, often transferable in principle | Summing discipline-specific prescriptions, assuming identical fatigue or response, exact maintenance fuel |
| Consensus A01, B09, C08, D01 | Contextual safety and practical ranges | Causal dose-response estimates or operational numerical thresholds |
| Measurement E01–E03 | Uncertainty in wearable and dietary observations | A correction factor, individualized TEE estimate, REDs prediction |
| Candidate policies §3–4 | Explicit, testable decision procedures | Evidence that those procedures improve physiological outcomes |

Important overlap audit: King/Henselmans share some resistance CHO trials; Temesi/Ramos-Campo and Tarrit's umbrella review are nested evidence, not independent confirmation. Burke's race-walker trials may be in Gejl/Nybo; compare inclusion lists. Hartono likely includes the acute concurrent protein trial and high-protein comparisons with varied control diets; inspect each eligibility criterion before pooling or citing. Morton may overlap protein timing and restriction syntheses; Witard reviews Kato and later IAAO work, not independent replication. Caldwell explicitly reuses previously published performance results; Oxfeldt papers on LEA and performance may share cohorts or protocols, verify before counting. Male Jurov studies may overlap. Publication checks beyond identified corrections and accessible bibliographic records remain outstanding. No formal sex-specific or dual-training moderation meta-analysis has been performed. An unreported CI is not zero uncertainty; p-values and Bayes factors have different interpretations. Clinical guidance must not be encoded as an empirical intervention effect.

## 3. Candidate coaching policies

All policies below are original programming/nutrition methodology unless a specific empirical finding is identified. They are pending review and not operational. Required inputs refer to the minimum for the stated action, not all possible observations. No evidence-derived numerical target is yet an approved parameter. All material revisions to training remain subject to athlete approval as in Packets 001–003.

### 3.1 Coverage and safety precedence

| Level | Observed data | Permitted candidate actions | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Training history and current schedule, no nutrition observations | Ask about meal access, preferences, recent fueling, goals and symptoms; offer general educational fueling options; assess training with previous policies. | No claim about actual intake, macros, deficit, EA or TEE. |
| L2 | L1 plus daily calories and body mass | Review intake/weight trend coverage, possible mismatch as a hypothesis; clarify completeness and unintentional changes. | No inference of macro composition or meal timing; no direct EA/TEE estimate. |
| L3 | L2 plus daily macros | Compare sufficiently complete days against contextual reference ranges if appropriate; assess tradeoffs and ask about timing. | No inference of within-day availability or exact requirements. |
| L4 | L3 plus meal-level timestamps and amounts | Examine plausible timing mismatches and plan practical meals/snacks around training with athlete agreement. | No direct glycogen measurement, clinical diagnosis or certainty about adherence. |

These are capability classes, not a rigid ladder. E.g., meal timing self-report without calorie counts can still support a qualitative suggestion. A given day or nutrient can have different coverage. All levels require the athlete's preferences, consent, restrictions and a usable training prescription for a personalized proposal. Label source, completeness and uncertainty separately for each variable. Missingness, partial days and duplicate records cannot be treated as measured zero. Explicitly reported zero is a different observation requiring context. Weight collection should be optional and not encouraged when unsafe or distressing. Do not require calorie tracking to participate in coaching. Safety screening takes precedence over optimization and even apparently complete data do not establish physiological adequacy.

### NU-P01 — Nutrition-aware training progression

- **Decision:** whether to increase, retain or reduce a planned workload, or clarify fueling first.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1: actual and planned strength and running exposure, objectives, comparable performance/effort, athlete feedback, constraints; L2+ adds intake and weight trend with coverage; L3/4 adds composition/timing. Recovery signals only as contextual observations.
- **Logic:** apply existing RT-P02 and concurrent policies; identify whether a proposed increase changes energy demand or meal opportunities. If a productive dose and no material concerns, retain or consider a bounded progression on its own merits. If there are credible signs of inadequate fueling, unexpected weight decline or deteriorating performance, ask about changes in intake, prior session fuel, actual training, sleep, illness and life stress. If unresolved, prefer holding progression, propose addressing feasible fueling or reducing workload where warranted; reassess after comparable exposures. Refer if concerning symptoms. If nutrition data are missing alone, do not block ordinary low-risk progression solely for that reason; disclose uncertainty and invite feedback.
- **Evidence/applicability:** A02/A03 controlled energy interventions, A05 resistance-training evidence; B01/B03 acute fueling; directness varies. Existing volume policy from Packet 002 remains intact.
- **Judgment and limits:** decision ordering and preference to avoid escalating an unresolved burden are safeguards, not tested superiority. Improvements in one domain do not rule out nutritional constraints in the other. An athlete may improve despite insufficient energy, and a performance decline may reflect measurement noise, inadequate stimulus or fatigue independent of diet.
- **Defer/contraindications:** persistent unexplained symptoms, suspected injury/illness, disordered eating concerns, clinician-prescribed diet or conflicting restrictions. No automatic nutritional diagnosis or load adjustment from a wearable reading.

### NU-P02 — Daily nutrition objectives conditioned on workload

- **Decision:** what dietary priorities to discuss across days and weeks, not an exact calorie prescription.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1 schedule (duration, intensity, working sets, muscle overlap, goal phase), preferences and access; L2 can review daily total trends; L3 with reliable body mass supports contextual macro comparisons. L4 is optional.
- **Logic:** identify priority sessions and overall demand; review energy adequacy first, ensure protein and dietary fat/variety, then plan carbohydrate availability to support work and next sessions. Higher-demand days may need additional eating opportunities; rest days still support tissue repair and glycogen restoration. Compare complete intake observations against appropriate reference ranges in §2.2–2.4 only as education/review context, not pass/fail. If ranges conflict with energy constraints, surface tradeoffs and propose revising goals or seeking dietitian support rather than inventing a macro optimum. Avoid double counting session fuel in daily totals.
- **Evidence/applicability:** B09/D01 consensus, C05–C08, A02/A05; no validated joint target function.
- **Judgment/limits:** timing daily targets to training is plausible methodology; strength-session duration alone cannot map to endurance guidance. No universal calories/kg training multiplier, macro percentages or automatic wearable eat-back. Adequate intake may come from ordinary foods.
- **Defer:** unreliable totals, missing mass for mass-relative calculations, pregnancy, diabetes/medications affecting glucose, renal/metabolic disease or other conditions needing specialized advice. Provide general principles or referral instead of individualized numerical targets.

### NU-P03 — Session-specific carbohydrate fueling

- **Decision:** whether to suggest pre-, during- or post-session CHO planning.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1 planned session duration/intensity, priority, previous/next sessions, prior meal timing by voluntary report, tolerance, logistical options. L4 supports individualized timing review but is not required for generic options.
- **Logic:** distinguish brief/low-demand fed sessions from high-volume lifting, sustained hard running, long runs and race-specific sessions. If prior intake is unclear, ask rather than infer fasting. Offer familiar carbohydrate foods/drinks when demand and timing warrant; use §2.2 ranges as contextual references only. Discuss gastrointestinal tolerance, gradual rehearsal and access to water; avoid introducing unfamiliar race-day strategies. Post-session options depend on time to next demanding work, prior intake and ordinary meal plan. Do not prescribe routine train-low sessions on the premise of better endurance adaptation.
- **Evidence/applicability:** B01/B02 resistance acute outcomes; B03/B04 endurance; B05 glycogen; B07/B08 restriction; B09 consensus. Direct hybrid dose and timing evidence sparse.
- **Judgment/limits:** individualized fueling opportunities, priority order, tolerance; no inference that carbohydrate increases long-term hypertrophy because it increases repetitions. No fixed drink requirement by duration; reported ranges are not machine thresholds.
- **Defer:** GI symptoms needing medical assessment, glucose management conditions, uncertain safety, athlete declines or cannot access food; consider a schedule or workload change instead of assuming intake can be forced.

### NU-P04 — Protein adequacy and distribution

- **Decision:** whether to discuss increasing total protein, adjusting distribution, or leaving intake unchanged.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1 training and goals for general guidance; L3 with representative complete protein data and measured body mass for comparison; L4 or self-report for meal timing. Dietary pattern and preferences; restriction status.
- **Logic:** first consider adequacy of total energy and CHO; compare complete daily protein in appropriate units with population evidence and existing pattern. If plausibly low, explore achievable food-based changes; if already adequate and outcomes satisfactory, do not automatically increase. Discuss distributing protein-containing meals/snacks and pairing with recovery food as convenient. If already consuming substantial protein, consider whether additional protein displaces needed CHO or fat. Avoid inferring exact individual requirement from a breakpoint or adding strength and endurance targets.
- **Evidence/applicability:** C01/C02 direct concurrent supplementation evidence, C04 non-dose experiment, C05/C06 RT, C07/C08 endurance, C09/C10 restriction, C11/C12 timing; A02 high protein does not remove adverse effects of LEA.
- **Judgment/limits:** totals-first hierarchy and optional meal distribution are proposed methodology. No compulsory immediate post-workout window, fixed grams per meal, supplementation mandate, or claim that more protein increases VO₂max.
- **Defer:** missing protein/mass data, renal/metabolic conditions, eating disorder risk or dietary restrictions requiring clinician/dietitian input; no forced tracking.

### NU-P05 — Double-session coordination

- **Decision:** how to integrate food/recovery opportunities and priority session quality on days with two sessions.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1 ordered sessions, duration/intensity, overlapping muscle demands, actual inter-session time, travel/work and meal access, preferences, prior meal/fueling report; L4 optional.
- **Logic:** if both sessions are demanding, protect the higher-priority work and plan achievable recovery food (carbohydrate plus protein as appropriate) between them. Distinguish two easy/low-overlap sessions from depletion-sensitive combinations. If recovery time is short and substantial glycogen repletion is plausible, discuss rapid-refueling evidence B05/B09; do not enforce its numerical range indiscriminately. If fueling opportunity is inadequate or GI constraints make the plan impractical, offer a different session order, lower secondary burden or separate-day schedule as in Packet 003. Count all session intake once in day totals. Reassess completed session quality.
- **Evidence/applicability:** B05/B06 glycogen restoration; B01/B03 acute performance; concurrent timing evidence in Packet 003. No direct evidence establishes optimum combined fueling plus schedule.
- **Judgment/limits:** feasible eating opportunities and priority-session protection; no mandatory gap, arbitrary glycogen model or assumption that an upper-body lift depletes leg glycogen comparably to a long run.
- **Defer:** athlete reports faintness, injury/illness, repeated severe GI intolerance or clinical nutrition needs; clarify or obtain qualified assessment.

### NU-P06 — Intentional body-composition goals and energy restriction

- **Decision:** whether a weight-change objective is compatible with the current block or should be revised/deferred.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1 explicitly athlete-chosen goal, phase, event timing, training history and symptoms; L2+ coverage/trend if athlete agrees and monitoring is appropriate; relevant clinical guidance where applicable.
- **Logic:** establish goal priority and clarify tradeoffs. Avoid endorsing aggressive restriction concurrent with escalating high-demand training, race preparation or concerning symptoms. If athlete and qualified professional elect a weight-management phase, preserve key training stimulus and adequate fueling; evaluate whether one or more performance objectives should become maintenance rather than simultaneous maximization. Review actual performance, adherence and well-being; propose deferring weight loss when objectives conflict, rather than silently cutting food or removing training. For gain phases, discuss uncertain benefit of larger surpluses and potential unwanted mass gain, particularly for running; no mandatory surplus for all strength blocks. Do not derive a deficit target from reported weight or wearable calories.
- **Evidence/applicability:** A02/A03 LEA effects; A05–A08 RT/athlete weight-change evidence; C09/C10 restriction protein. No randomized hybrid goal-priority algorithm.
- **Judgment/limits:** phase preference and preserving training quality are candidate risk-management approaches. The research doses are not recommended diets. Weight is not a required objective; avoid appearance pressure or presenting weight loss as inherently beneficial for running.
- **Defer/contraindications:** minors outside scope, pregnancy, suspected REDs, disordered eating, unintentional loss or medical conditions affecting weight; stop body-composition optimization and refer. Avoid numeric goals where they may reinforce harm.

### NU-P07 — Concerning patterns: clarify, protect and refer

- **Decision:** ordinary coaching review vs qualified assessment, without diagnosis or severity scoring.
- **Inputs/minimum:** L1 volunteered symptoms, health restrictions, repeated changes in performance, training/food context and athlete concerns; any additional observations optional. Do not solicit unnecessary intimate or sensitive data.
- **Logic:** if reports suggest persistent unexplained fatigue, recurrent injury/illness, suspected bone stress injury, menstrual changes where relevant, changes in libido, substantial unintentional weight change or disordered eating concerns, express uncertainty and recommend sports medicine/registered sports dietitian evaluation; stop proposing weight-loss/escalation while unresolved. For acute severe symptoms (e.g., fainting during exertion, chest pain or severe breathing difficulty), advise urgent medical assessment/emergency services as appropriate, not a fueling trial. Do not wait for abnormal weight or wearable values to take a report seriously. Maintain or restrict activity only within professional guidance and immediate safety constraints; no automated return-to-sport clearance.
- **Evidence/applicability:** A01 clinical consensus; A02/A03 physiology; male evidence A04. These are warning patterns, not a validated screening list or risk calculator.
- **Judgment/limits:** conservative safety boundary; referral is not diagnosis. Other causes include infection, anemia, endocrine disease, medication, psychosocial stress and training errors. No CAT2 traffic-light recreation, EA cutoff, automated REDs label or inference from sex/menstrual status alone.
- **Defer:** all suspected clinical conditions to qualified professionals; athlete can decline data sharing without loss of ordinary nonclinical support. Document only necessary user-authorized observations in private owner-scoped storage, not shared corpus.

### NU-P08 — Nutrition data integrity and disagreement

- **Decision:** whether intake/weight/EE data can support an interpretation, and what information to request.
- **Inputs/minimum:** any coverage level; provenance, day boundaries, completeness, source duplication, timestamps, measurement method and revision state if present.
- **Logic:** preserve unknown, partial, estimated and explicitly reported values separately. Resolve mirrored Garmin/MFP/Health Connect summaries without double counting; flag conflicting records, do not choose a provider merely because values seem plausible. Compare trends on aligned dates with coverage disclosed, and ask whether recorded intake reflects actual intake. If wearable EE disagrees with observed weight trend, report both as uncertain and investigate context rather than correcting either by a fixed factor. Offer qualitative fueling guidance or defer quantitative review when prerequisites are missing. Never assume lack of a food log is lack of eating.
- **Evidence/applicability:** E01–E03 measurement limitations; A01 EA assessment. The ingest and missingness rules are engineering safeguards rather than intervention effects.
- **Judgment/limits:** data sufficiency and comparability must be reviewed and versioned; no arbitrary completeness threshold, calorie correction, personalized TDEE estimator or modeled EA output. Packet 007 owns model specification.
- **Defer:** corrupted, ambiguous, duplicate or inadequately attributed records; safety concerns trigger NU-P07 regardless of coverage.

### 3.2 Review procedure across policies (candidate)

1. Resolve immediate safety/medical concerns and user consent; honor restrictions. Separate clinical referral from ordinary plan selection.
2. Freeze actual and proposed training exposures, goals, phase, food/weight coverage, relevant feedback, and provenance. Determine which questions are answerable at current coverage.
3. Evaluate comparable performance and adherence in both disciplines. Consider multiple competing explanations; do not label low energy or interference from one observation.
4. Choose feasible alternatives: maintain, clarify, offer fueling changes, modify timing, reduce workload, progress, or revise block objective. Prefer a productive dose when evidence for a change is weak (Packet 002); make tradeoffs explicit.
5. If safe and agreed, propose a specific reversible change with desired outcomes and observation plan. Do not adjust multiple variables unnecessarily when interpretation matters; safety may require simultaneous changes. No causality inferred from a single before/after comparison.
6. Revalidate block constraints, show prior versus proposed schedule and rationale; secure explicit athlete approval for prescription changes. Reassess using completed work, comparability and missingness. Evidence citations must be eligible from an independently reviewed release before making operational empirical assertions; current packet has none.

## 4. Periodization methodology

### 4.1 Three coupled horizons

- **Session:** eating opportunity and carbohydrate availability before priority sessions; intake during prolonged work if appropriate; recovery food aligned with next work. This is where timing may matter most (B01, B03, B05).
- **Day:** total intake, nutrient balance, food access and recovery needs; use conditional reference ranges only when input and clinical context permit. A rest day may follow depletion and precede hard training; do not infer low needs from absence of activity alone.
- **Block:** goals for strength, hypertrophy, running, health, body composition, adherence; review multiweek trends and schedule changes. Energy sufficiency and compatible objectives take priority over exact daily matching. Distinguish tolerable tradeoffs from unsafe constraints; nutrition should not become an independently optimized plan.

| Block archetype (Packet 003) | Nutritional priorities and day structure | Body composition and energy constraints | Adaptation to schedule change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength emphasis | Sufficient energy to support priority lift adaptation, protein across day, carbohydrate for high-volume lifting and demanding runs; replenish on recovery days. Maintain appropriate fueling for secondary endurance quality. | Hypertrophy goal may motivate discussing gain, but no automatic surplus; maximal strength need not require mass gain, and gain can trade off with running. Avoid restricting food solely because run volume reduced. | If adding sets, review total burden and feasible food access before increasing both run and lift load. If replacing an easy run with intervals, review priority-session and between-session fueling. |
| Endurance emphasis | Protect carbohydrate availability for long/threshold/interval sessions and race-specific practice; sufficient energy and protein to support endurance remodeling and maintenance lifting. | Do not pursue lower body mass automatically as mileage rises. An athlete may maintain or increase mass while improving endurance; unintentional loss prompts review. | If mileage/duration increases, review meal timing/total intake and recovery; adjust food opportunities rather than exact wearable calorie compensation. If strength maintenance session falls near hard run, review sequence and recovery meals. |
| Balanced development | Coordinate demanding days, avoid displacing CHO with excessive protein, and protect both disciplines. Ordinary meals plus planned snacks when useful. | Simultaneously maximizing muscle gain, fast running, fat loss and adherence may be infeasible. Clarify acceptable secondary-objective tradeoffs. | If time constraints force two sessions together, NU-P05 compares schedule and fueling alternatives. Do not increase both modalities and assume diet can compensate. |

### 4.2 Phase-specific considerations

| Phase / event | Illustrative methodology, not validated prescription |
|---|---|
| Accumulation / higher-volume weeks | Reassess food access and sustained energy demand; place CHO around demanding sessions, maintain adequate protein and fat. Review actual exposures and appetite, not only planned load. |
| Intensification | Lower set counts may coincide with higher loads, harder intervals or race-specific demands. Preserve fuel for quality; do not mechanically reduce calories when set count drops. |
| Deload / recovery | Recovery and tissue remodeling continue. Review actual workload, appetite and goals; avoid reflex restriction or treating reduced activity as a requirement to lose weight. |
| Running race taper | Protect glycogen and rehearsal of familiar intake; context-specific loading B09 for prolonged events, not all races. Expect transient glycogen/water changes; do not cut calories in response to scale increase. |
| Strength assessment | Retain ordinary meals and adequate carbohydrate/energy; avoid unfamiliar diet or unnecessary carbohydrate loading, unless broader training/event context warrants it. |
| Intentional weight change | Separate or reduce competing priorities if appropriate; consider timing away from demanding event preparation, with qualified support. Monitor health, training quality and adherence without assuming an optimal loss/gain rate. |
| Temporary interruption | Actual exposure may fall while injury recovery or illness changes needs. Do not equate inactivity with a mandate for energy restriction; coordinate with clinician where relevant. Resume fueling for returning workload rather than preemptively escalating training. |

**Example (no prescribed diet):** A runner-lifter initially schedules heavy squats before an evening interval workout. The coach compares moving intervals to another day versus retaining both with an available recovery meal; asks about preceding breakfast/lunch and gut tolerance, and prioritizes the block objective. If intervals become the key session, protect that quality and avoid gratuitous lower-body volume. When a long run replaces an easy run, reconsider prior food and recovery opportunities. If weight unexpectedly declines and both performance measures worsen, hold escalation, consider multiple causes, and seek professional help when appropriate. None of these choices requires computing a daily calorie target from a watch.

**Scientific boundary:** the three horizons and phase examples are programming choices informed by A02/A03/B05/B07/B09/C05–C12. No retrieved trial compares the full three-archetype nutrition-aware strategy to independently generated training/nutrition plans. A desirable joint strategy must demonstrate feasibility and eventually benefit, not just cite plausible mechanisms.

### 6.1 Evaluation design and metrics

Conformance: paired cases (N01–N04, N05–N06, N07–N08, N15–N16, N18–N19) isolate information or goal changes. Count missingness errors, unsupported inferences, incorrect evidence citations or transfers, unsafe/contraindicated advice, ignored restrictions, duplicate calorie counts, unapproved mutations and failure to escalate concerning symptoms. For each case record acceptable alternatives, required disclosures, forbidden recommendations, expected persistence and absence of writes. A critical safety, privacy or authority violation blocks release. Passing deterministic tests does not validate physiological benefit.

Later prospective evaluation (only after expert review and participant consent): compare a simple coordinated training baseline with general nutrition education versus a nutrition-aware adaptive coaching policy. A possible additional comparison is individualized dietitian-led standard care; do not withhold indicated clinical care or assign harmful underfueling. Stratify by baseline training histories, goal archetype, and optional data coverage. Prespecify outcomes: comparable priority-lift strength and running performance, adherence and completed training quality, fatigue and well-being, nutrition burden and user experience, and adverse events/referrals. Weight/body-composition outcomes only if athlete opts in and clinically appropriate. Report attrition, missingness, adherence and measurement uncertainty, not just mean change among completers. Distinguish an improved decision process from improved physiology, and account for training or life changes. Acute within-athlete comparisons of safe, tolerable fueling opportunities may help refine hypotheses but not prove chronic benefit or establish a REDs diagnosis. Do not deliberately induce problematic LEA. Packet 009 will design prospective comparisons, statistical estimands and stopping rules with scientific/clinical reviewers.

## 8. Human review checklist

### 8.1 Scientific extraction and interpretation

- [ ] Read original publications for all proposed claims and exact result locators. Confirm all denominators, sample sizes, intervention durations, units, effect signs, confidence versus credible intervals, and comparator definitions; verify ± notation before interpreting it as SD/SE.
- [ ] Oxfeldt: verify randomization, adherence, training experience in both modalities, diet composition, interaction effect and CI; confirm the distinction between integrated myofibrillar synthesis and chronic hypertrophy/strength. Verify sarcoplasmic result separately if extracting it.
- [ ] Caldwell: verify achieved EA, period/carryover and dietary controls, cycling test and between-condition versus within-condition effects; identify earlier report and prevent duplicate evidence weighting. Check whether recovery period was sufficient to interpret findings.
- [ ] Murphy/Koehler: reconcile abstract −0.57 versus body −0.58 lean-mass estimate; extract actual intervals, population and quality for parallel contrasts. Do not conflate Analysis B matched studies with randomization or use fitted zero-gain intercept as a safe deficit.
- [ ] Male LEA studies: obtain full texts, verify altered exercise volume and potential fatigue confounding, overlap and outcome-specific evidence. Do not extrapolate female cutoff to males or vice versa.
- [ ] Garthe/Helms: verify assigned versus achieved weight-change rates/surpluses, intervention lengths and adherence, training stimulus, strength results and skinfold/lean mass interpretation; do not ignore Helms bench result or interpret null effects as equivalence.
- [ ] King/Henselmans: audit overlapping studies, acute endpoints, fasting and sensory/isocaloric controls, maximal-effort protocols, sex and training status; confirm subgroup precision and that reported duration/set categories are not prescription cutoffs.
- [ ] Craven: verify dry-muscle units, carbohydrate/control differences, recovery window, protein comparator and heterogeneity; no direct inference of subsequent performance from glycogen restoration alone.
- [ ] Endurance CHO reviews: obtain full text of Temesi/Ramos-Campo and newer umbrella review; check running-specific trials, TT versus exhaustion, control diets, dose, duration, and overlap. Check original recent very-high-dose trials before making any >90 g/h discussion more than screening context.
- [ ] Gejl/Nybo and Burke: confirm train-low strategy definitions and energy matching, participant selection, performance versus mechanistic outcomes, and overlapping trial cohorts; no routine low-CHO prescription. Do not treat within-group changes as randomized between-group differences.
- [ ] Hartono: obtain full text and primary concurrent studies; extract actual estimates/CIs where available and distinguish review counts from pooled treatment effects. Verify Camera and high-protein comparative study; latter does not randomize protein exposure. Determine which samples are genuinely experienced in both disciplines.
- [ ] Morton: confirm duration eligibility, pooling units, subgroup definitions and Fig. 5 model/uncertainty; distinguish study-arm breakpoint from individual requirement. Verify Kato sample, dose-response method and newer IAAO studies; avoid conflating upper confidence bound with a universal minimum.
- [ ] Longland/Mettler: verify DOI/metadata, protein units, energy and macronutrient matching, adherence, lean mass and performance endpoints; severe experimental restrictions are not coaching recommendations. Areta/Schoenfeld: confirm acute versus chronic distinction and newer evidence before any timing rule.
- [ ] Dietary fat and consensus ranges: verify original Academy and MSSE parallel publication and erratum, context, population and units. Consider newer sports nutrition guidance; ranges are not numerical policy defaults. Check food practicality, dietary variety and clinical exclusions with qualified dietitian.
- [ ] Wearable and food-log validation: verify E01–E03 exact denominators, criteria, comparator, error distributions, DLW assumptions, current device protocols and version applicability. Do not use study-average errors to correct individual records.
- [ ] Complete source correction/retraction/version checks, study-level risk-of-bias, overlap audit and rights/permission review for intended deployment. No copyrighted figures, article text or tables should be bundled beyond permitted use; retain links and original summaries.
