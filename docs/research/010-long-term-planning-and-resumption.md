# Research Packet 010 — Long-Term Planning, Interruptions, Resumption and Adherence

**Fitness Intelligence** · Revision 0.1 · 2026-11-25 · Pending independent human review  
**Repository:** <https://github.com/dkiv/fitness-intelligence>  
**Scope:** Experienced recreational hybrid athletes; single-user personal coaching over months and years.  
**Dependencies:** Packets 001–009, standing observation-aware methodology, and separately reviewed J3 methodology. This is the final scheduled preliminary packet, not an operational release or an authorization to implement rules.

## 1. Executive summary

The long-term planning layer should organize the athlete's intentions and constraints for the existing planner, not replace that planner or prescribe an entire year of numerical workloads. A productive block may continue; a disrupted block need not be restarted; and a new goal does not erase historical training evidence.

- **Plan at different levels of commitment.** Goals, priorities and fixed dates constrain a provisional sequence of blocks. The current approved block specifies outcome commitments and dose bounds. Detailed prescriptions are generated close to execution and approved; distant blocks stay conditional.
- **Treat interruptions as changes in opportunity, exposure and sometimes capacity.** A missed session is not a debt. Retained running, lifting and other activity matter separately. Neither absence of an import nor passage of a particular number of days establishes detraining.
- **Resume from current credible references.** Existing retention studies and new strength detraining evidence support neither a universal decay rate nor a universal restart fraction. A reduced, familiar introductory exposure can provide a current comparison without requiring maximal testing. Clinical restrictions take precedence over any history.
- **Design for actual adherence.** Repeated missed training is a reason to check time, preferences, burden, constraints and record coverage, not to label the athlete unmotivated. Flexible alternatives and shared decisions are reasonable candidate methods, not guaranteed adherence interventions.
- **Review outcomes rather than arbitrary dates.** End-of-block review may justify continuation, extension, transition, assessment or recovery. Preserve productive components and explicitly renegotiate commitments that no longer fit.

The targeted new evidence includes a supervised interrupted resistance-training comparison in previously untrained adults, a short detraining study in trained men, and exercise-attendance research on planning, flexibility and autonomy. It complements rather than duplicates Packet 006's maintenance/taper evidence and Packet 009's historical reasoning. Direct comparisons of complete rolling-horizon hybrid planning or different return-to-training algorithms in experienced dual-trained recreational athletes were not identified in this targeted search. Practical choices may be reviewed and used conservatively without claiming comparative physiological superiority.

The preliminary roadmap now closes after Packet 010. J3 curation, observation verification, practical programming parameters and implementation validation remain separate and unfinished. Future investigations should be triggered by a specific unresolved coaching decision, observation limitation or failure in actual use.

### 2.5 Measurement and observation appraisal

The scientific construct is independent of a provider; the field measurement is only eligible if its coverage, provenance and comparison conditions are appropriate. The following are methodological assessments building on P005/007/008/009, not new treatment-effect claims.

| Construct | Reference / practical canonical observation | Validity, reliability, sensitivity and confounding | Decision availability and fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retained maximal strength / exercise skill | Standardized measured 1RM; familiar submaximal load, reps, reported effort | Repeatability is protocol-dependent; technique, equipment, effort and fatigue matter. Estimated 1RM is not measured strength. No single test establishes repeated-exposure tolerance. | Conditionally feasible; use familiar introductory work with feedback, or avoid numeric escalation when reference stale. |
| Aerobic capacity / running performance / durability | Laboratory VO2max, standardized endurance test, race; comparable actual runs, effort and late-session tolerance | Distinct outcomes; field pace confounded by heat, terrain and HR quality. Short run cannot verify long-duration retention. Aggregate splits or instructions do not establish achieved intervals. | Conditionally feasible for performance; laboratory constructs deferred. Use easy, contextual guidance without precise capacity estimates. |
| Exposure and adherence | Direct observed execution; canonical activity, matched sets, actual durations, athlete confirmation | Inferred completion and missing imports differ from known missed work. Calendar and activity type do not establish effort or dose. | Feasible with eligible actuals; otherwise label unknown and ask only if decision depends on it. |
| Tolerance and readiness | No universal gold standard; repeated execution, local/general feedback, comparable performance | Task-specific, subjective scale and reporting drift, illness, nutrition, life stress. Completion is not optimality, pain is not a readiness-score problem. | Conditionally feasible; hold or choose reversible modification; clinical concerns defer affected training. |
| Sustained participation / preference | Recorded attendance and prescriptions, athlete's confirmed goals and experiences | Attendance is not adherence; preference changes over time; avoid inferring motivation from missing sessions. | Feasible to ask and record a correction; long-term benefit remains descriptive. |
| Nutritional feasibility | Controlled intake/expenditure if available; reported fueling, intake/weight trends and feedback | Missing logs not zero, energy estimates biased, mass affected by fluid, no direct energy availability measure. | Qualitative support feasible; quantitative adjustment only under P007 observation eligibility and stable logging conditions. |

**Mutable engineering snapshot:** Hevy strength history and Garmin activity summaries, optional athlete feedback and approved plans provide a plausible foundation. Historical coverage, actual session matching, device/protocol changes, RPE and calorie/weight completeness are not guaranteed. Unknown HRV units, absent high-resolution telemetry and optional macro/meal data do not prevent otherwise defensible qualitative advice. Do not use provider identifiers in scientific records. Richer observations may improve comparison quality, but do not establish that a different long-term policy improves outcomes.

## 3. Candidate coaching policies

Seven distinct decisions are proposed. They extend rather than replace P006 HP-P01/02/03/08/09, P008 RE-P09/11 and P009 IA-P01–06, plus CT-P02/05, RT-P01/02 and EN-P01/02. All are revision **0.1**, pending independent human review; scientific readiness, data readiness and operational authorization are separate. No policy is operationally approved. Feasibility labels describe observation supportability only. In each entry P = prescription/recommendation, A = adherence assessment, E = effectiveness/outcome assessment. Ideal and optional observations need not be collected simply because they exist.

### LT-P01 — Rolling-horizon intentions

- **Decision / constructs / support:** choose current block and provisional successors given goals, specificity and event constraints; builds on P006 periodization and maintenance evidence, not a tested rolling-horizon algorithm. Practical assumption: less commitment to distant doses avoids needless replanning.
- **Observations:** minimum P: confirmed priorities, restrictions, current schedule and credible habitual exposure, known important dates. Ideal: comparable strength/endurance references and reliable calendars. Optional: prior block responses and event flexibility. A: actual approved session outcomes. E: objective-specific trends and burden, not whether tentative future blocks occurred.
- **Logic:** retain a small set of conditional intentions with reason, prerequisites, protected outcomes and review trigger. Invoke HP-P01/02 for current block; specify near-term work only. At review, update tentative successors without changing the current approved block unless a material conflict requires it.
- **Readiness/fallback:** scientific: indirect evidence plus programming judgment. P feasible for intentions, conditional for quantified prescription; A/E conditional on history. Unknown event or recent exposure → provisional plan or clarification, not invented dates/doses. Prohibited: universal block duration, yearly optimum, implied approval of future sessions. Richer data can improve estimates of feasible sessions, not guarantee trajectory. Approval: explicit acceptance for changed goals or active block; provisional discussion is read-only.

### LT-P02 — Continue, extend or transition (extends HP-P09)

- **Decision / support:** judge whether current block still serves its outcome commitments; P002 maintenance-dose, P006 retention/periodization and P009 comparable-history methods support considering continuity; no demonstrated optimal transition trigger.
- **Observations:** minimum P: current objective, completed exposure, constraints, available outcome evidence and athlete intent. Ideal: comparable endpoint and secondary measures with tolerance and adherence. Optional: fatigue, nutritional context and prior blocks. A: actual dose relative to in-force plan. E: relevant outcomes, uncertainty and next-phase review.
- **Logic:** if productive, feasible and desired, offer continuation; a calendar endpoint is a review opportunity, not a reason to overhaul. If objective met, select next meaningful aim; if unmet, distinguish low execution, weak measurement, excessive burden, poor specificity, goal change. Extend only if useful and event-compatible; assess if a measurement would change the next block. Preserve movements/doses that still serve goals; use HP-P07/08 only when recovery or assessment requires it.
- **Readiness/fallback:** P conditional, A/E conditional; scientific indirect. Insufficient observations → no claim of stagnation, hold or request feedback. No mandatory tests/deloads, causal attribution, or replacing exercises for novelty. Richer comparable outcomes improve review. Approval: extension changing block dates/bounds or transition needs block revision; unchanged continuation within approved horizon does not.

### LT-P03 — Missed session or interrupted week

- **Decision / support:** skip, redistribute or revise remaining exposure; P003 priority scheduling and P006 block protection, indirect; no direct trial of missed-work repayment. Guardrail: maintain feasibility and don't automatically repay missed sets or mileage.
- **Observations:** minimum P: approved slots, confirmed completion/partial/miss/unknown, remaining availability, restrictions, objectives, neighboring sessions. Ideal actual load, effort and retained sport exposure. Optional fatigue and schedule flexibility. A: match actual work to original prescription; E: subsequent priority-session quality, burden and secondary commitments.
- **Logic:** freeze elapsed work. Preserve future priority sessions; consider moving an individual session only if time, dose bounds and relevant adjacent-session constraints pass. Drop lower-priority discretionary work rather than stacking. A missed protected commitment is a deviation; if no feasible way to preserve future commitment, surface conflict and seek explicit block review. Do not retroactively mark skipped work completed or rewrite approved history.
- **Readiness/fallback:** P feasible for confirmed interruption; A/E conditional. Missing imports → unknown, ask if consequential. No automatic debt, detraining diagnosis or motivational judgment. More granular actuals could resolve partial exposures. Approval: keep plan/read-only if unchanged; reschedule/change prescription requires weekly approval; changing commitments requires block revision.

### LT-P04 — Conservative resumption (extends IA-P03/05, HP-P02, RE-P03/05)

- **Decision / support:** choose credible starting exposure after reduced training, separately by task; L01–L03 and P006 maintenance/detraining evidence give directional context, not exact restart prescriptions. Assumption: a reversible introductory session can inform present tolerance without a maximal test.
- **Observations:** minimum P: reason and known duration, retained exposure by discipline, current restrictions and availability, last comparable references, athlete feedback about present ability or uncertainty. Ideal: recent task-specific submaximal performance and response over repeated sessions. Optional sleep, fueling, effort and environmental context. A: actual sets/reps/load, runs and deviations. E: comparable performance, subsequent tolerance, local/general symptoms and retained secondary outcomes.
- **Logic:** if only calendar changed and current references remain credible, retain ordinary work subject to feasibility. If a prior dose is questionable, choose familiar movement/session type and lower burden or difficulty than the former demanding prescription; use current effort/performance bounds and monitor subsequent response before adding exposure. If no credible reference, seek conservative introductory assessment with an eligible method rather than prescribe from an old PR. Keep unaffected training where feasible. A new clinical restriction overrides all history; defer affected training and seek appropriate professional guidance, not medical clearance from the coach.
- **Readiness/fallback:** P conditionally feasible for ordinary nonclinical resumption; quantitative prescription deferred without defensible reference or where clinical clearance is unresolved. A/E conditional. No universal loss rate, duration threshold, restart percentage, readiness diagnosis or equivalence of cross-training and running tolerance. Richer data may refine task-specific starting loads and durations. Approval: changed session outside approved execution choices needs proposal; changed bounds or block objective requires block revision.

### LT-P05 — Event and availability coordination (extends HP-P08, CT-P02)

- **Decision / support:** plan backward from fixed dates while preserving feasible joint commitments; P006 taper evidence indirect for hybrid coordination. Practical assumption: event priority and calendar feasibility constrain block choice.
- **Observations:** minimum P: event date, priority, fixed/flexible status, protected objectives, restrictions, available time and current plan. Ideal actual phase, relevant performance and preparation history. Optional travel logistics, fueling and competing event dates. A: actual event and preparation exposure. E: event-specific performance, secondary outcomes and burden.
- **Logic:** mark protected preparation/recovery windows according to reviewed contextual parameters; reserve space in provisional block intentions. Revalidate when dates change. Compare moving an assessment, reducing discretionary work, changing emphasis or accepting a lower expectation; do not simultaneously maximize both close events by default. Keep taper optional and separately review its dose.
- **Readiness/fallback:** P feasible for calendar-level reasoning, conditional for specific event preparation; A/E conditional. Unknown date/priority → conditional options. No universal spacing, automatic taper, or silent downgrade of secondary commitment. Richer event context may refine allocation. Approval: revised schedule requires weekly approval; changed target/priority/bounds requires context/block revision.

### LT-P06 — Adherence-aware adaptation

- **Decision / support:** distinguish a feasible prescription from one the athlete repeatedly cannot or does not want to execute; L04–L07 indirect and flexible programming evidence P006. Shared decisions and respecting preferences are product choices, not efficacy guarantees.
- **Observations:** minimum P: actual known deviations, schedule and time constraints, athlete-confirmed preferences or targeted explanation. Ideal repeated execution with durations and perceived burden; optional fatigue, environment, equipment and enjoyment feedback. A: completed/partial/missed/unknown by approved intent. E: actual exposure, practical satisfaction, performance and secondary commitments after the change.
- **Logic:** first check coverage and feasibility; ask a decision-relevant question rather than infer motive. Offer shorter compatible sessions, preferred equivalent exercises, alternative dates or lower-priority work reduction. Check specificity and maintenance implications. If desired participation no longer matches objectives, negotiate a revised objective rather than hiding the discrepancy.
- **Readiness/fallback:** P feasible for alternatives, conditional for dose changes; A/E conditional. Unknown reasons → nonjudgmental clarification or keep feasible plan. No psychological profile, permanent preference inference or guaranteed adherence improvement. Richer actual durations may improve estimates; don't require questionnaires. Approval: confirm preference updates; weekly changes require approval; changing outcome commitments requires block review.

### LT-P07 — Periodic objective review (extends IA-P04/06, HP-P09)

- **Decision / support:** periodically reconsider long-term direction based on observed outcomes and athlete intent. P009 descriptive history framework; no controlled trial validates review cadence.
- **Observations:** minimum P: existing objectives, current priorities/constraints, summary of completed blocks, athlete view; ideal comparable outcomes with actual exposure and historical rationales. Optional relevant nutrition/weight, tolerance and schedule patterns. A: actual completed work across blocks; E: progress against outcome commitments, coverage, satisfaction and burden over time.
- **Logic:** separate weekly dose review, end-of-block review and larger objective discussion. Ask whether goals remain meaningful; retain, reorder, replace or explicitly remove commitments. Recompute historical summaries, persist confirmed context and consequential decisions, mark unsupported patterns stale. Future intentions conditional on new context.
- **Readiness/fallback:** P feasible for conversation, conditional for quantified evaluation; A/E conditional. Incomplete records → disclose unknown outcomes rather than declare success/failure. No fixed review interval, individual causal response estimate or permanent motivation label. More comparable outcomes can refine direction. Approval: explicit context/objective revision and corresponding approved plan before changed commitments take effect.

## 4. Long-term planning, interruption and resumption methodology

### 4.1 Rolling horizon and hierarchy

| Horizon | Committed content | Conditional content / when to elaborate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-term direction | Athlete-confirmed priorities, restrictions, fixed events and acceptable tradeoffs | Performance targets with uncertain timing; revise explicitly when priorities or feasibility change. |
| Multi-block trajectory | Only agreed intentions/constraints; no future session authority | Candidate emphases, dependencies and assessment opportunities. Elaborate after current block review or meaningful event change. |
| Current approved block | Objective, secondary commitments, applicable restrictions, phase intent, reviewed dose bounds and review opportunities | Future week detail remains ungenerated; change block only with explicit approval. |
| Upcoming week | Approved dated prescriptions, recovery and event constraints | Next review may propose future changes; no silent mutation. |
| Session / execution | Exact approved prescription and allowed execution range | Athlete choices within range; actual deviations remain actuals, not retroactive prescription changes. |

Candidate configurations for J3: (A) athlete with fixed race date: one active block, tentative event-preparation successor and protected assessment opportunity; (B) athlete without fixed event: active balanced-development block, next emphasis intentionally undecided; (C) athlete with planned travel: active block, dated availability exception, provisional post-travel review before generating work. No universal number of months, blocks, weeks or review interval is justified. Dates and block lengths come from goals, actual schedule, approved context and reviewed programming convention.

### 4.3 Interruption decision framework

A calendar interruption changes opportunity. A dose interruption changes actual exposure. A capacity/tolerance concern makes a former prescription questionable. These can coexist, but the categories must not be inferred solely from elapsed time. First establish reason, retained work, restrictions, upcoming priorities, current availability and eligible performance. Apply the following branches in order of safety and feasibility; no threshold on interruption duration is implied.

| Condition | Candidate action | Review requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unresolved illness/injury restriction or concerning symptoms | Defer affected training and recommend qualified guidance; do not use HRV, prior PR or successful warm-up as clearance. Preserve unrelated safe choices only if appropriate. | Read-only advice/clinical referral; restrictions invalidate incompatible proposals and are not overridable by athlete preference. |
| Coverage insufficient to determine actual missed work | Mark unknown, request confirmation if the answer affects remaining prescription. | Read-only; do not create fictitious missed sessions. |
| Isolated missed work, current capacity credible | Continue scheduled work; skip missed exposure or selectively redistribute future work if useful and feasible. | No approval for unchanged plan; any changed prescription needs approval. |
| Calendar/equipment changed but retained capacity plausible | Substitute familiar equivalent work if appropriate; preserve priority and disclose specificity limitations. | Weekly proposal; block review if commitment or bounds no longer fit. |
| Several sessions missed or exposure materially reduced | Review actual exposure by discipline; consider temporarily reduced burden, introductory work, or new submaximal reference. | Weekly proposal within bounds; otherwise block revision. |
| Prolonged absence, current reference stale, or new availability incompatible | Reconstruct the block from current feasible schedule and credible introductory observations, preserving old history; defer unsupported quantification. | Explicit block revision, athlete approval. |
| Upcoming event compromises recovery or resumption | Protect or renegotiate event and secondary priorities; consider deferring optional assessment. | Context/block revision when goal or protected constraints change. |

**Contextual numerical choices for J3:** select introductory load using the most recent eligible submaximal reference and a reviewed effort range; select a lower burden by reducing familiar sets or repetitions; select running duration/intensity from retained actual running and current tolerance, not peak historical mileage. Increase only after eligible execution/tolerance and relevant objectives support a change. If safe bounds cannot be determined, use qualitative guidance or seek appropriate assessment. Do not set a universal resumption fraction, weekly mileage ramp, mandatory reassessment delay, loss rate or maximum gap between sessions. The 2- and 10-week study interruptions in section 2 are experimental exposures, not decision cutoffs. Choose exercise-specific increments, time budgets, event buffers and maintenance floors separately through J3; document alternatives and test feasibility at the bounds.

### 4.6 Practical evaluation for personal use

- **Software correctness:** frozen as-of context; no future information leakage; preserve approved history and old rationale; missing != missed; correct remaining time/dose calculations; no double-counted exposure; hard restriction invalidates stale candidate; unchanged approved plan after decline; equivalent canonical inputs produce equivalent decision. Check across a transition and an interruption, not only isolated weeks.
- **Recommendation quality:** reviewer checks whether continuation is a real option, whether the current block remains aligned with goals, whether competing commitments are explicit, whether credible current references drive re-entry, whether alternatives fit actual schedule, whether explanations disclose uncertainty and whether athlete authority is respected. Accept multiple defensible outcomes.
- **Personal usefulness:** at routine weekly and block reviews record actual completed work, known coverage, burden, practical fit, preferred alternative and comparable outcomes. Track repeated catch-up proposals, unnecessary plan churn, recurring infeasible slots and avoidable tracking burden. If a proposed policy repeatedly fails, compare a simpler shadow recommendation and investigate the precise failure. No formal experiment or population inference required. Satisfaction or improved performance is not proof of general causal effectiveness.
