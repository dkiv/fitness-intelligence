# Coaching science and evaluation

These requirements apply to the planned coach, beginning with its first seven-day plan.
A reviewer should be able to answer three questions about a recommendation. What does the
research support? Which programming choices involve coaching judgment? What has this athlete
actually experienced?

## Evidence and applicability

| Input | How the coach may use it | Limits to communicate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empirical evidence from peer-reviewed exercise science | For a question about intervention effects, start with relevant systematic reviews of controlled trials, then individual controlled trials. Use observational studies for associations and descriptive questions. | Assess study quality, risk of bias, consistency, precision and directness. A synthesis of weak or poorly matched studies can still warrant little confidence. |
| Practical methodology | Identify a coach's method, expert opinion or example program and use it as one candidate for organizing sessions. | Label the source and assumptions. Any assertion about effectiveness needs separate empirical support. |
| Individual response | Use completed sessions, adherence, measured trends and athlete feedback to tailor a decision. | Record timing, coverage and plausible alternative explanations. A within-athlete correlation can motivate a trial adjustment but cannot establish causation. |

For each empirical claim, record a stable claim ID and revision, the question, population,
intervention or exposure, comparator, outcome, observation period, result and uncertainty.
Attach a full citation, DOI or stable source link, and a locator for the supporting result.
Record study design, limitations, reviewer, review date and an applicability judgment. For
example, evidence about short-term strength in novice lifters needs an explicit transfer
argument before it supports a hybrid athlete's competition block.

Link conflicting results to the same question and compare their populations, protocols and
outcomes. If they remain inconsistent, retain both and state what remains uncertain. Where
direct support is missing, identify the judgment involved, offer a conservative option or
defer the adjustment. A citation must support the specific assertion in the explanation.

## Corpus stewardship

A curator selects sources for a defined planning question, checks publication and correction
status, and documents inclusion or exclusion. Record rights and permitted uses for each
source. Use bibliographic records, links and original summaries by default. Store or reproduce
article text, figures or proprietary program material only with documented permission or a
license covering that use. A program reference can identify a method without copying its
session sequence.

Review claims, applicability, conflicts and rights before publishing a versioned corpus release.
Revisit claims on scheduled review and when new evidence, corrections or retractions arise.
Supersede or withdraw affected claims, record the reason and identify rules and recommendations
that cited them. Exclude withdrawn claims from new advice. Keep version history and permitted
provenance so earlier decisions remain explainable. Re-evaluate affected plans and present any
proposed revision for approval.

## Periodization decisions

For each block, record why its approach fits the athlete's goal, time until the target event,
constraints, applicable evidence and prior response. The planner must support multiple approaches.
Compare feasible options against those criteria. Reassess the choice as the block unfolds.
The first coach can plan just the next seven days in detail, provided it knows the enclosing
cycle, block objective, intended progression and upcoming recovery or event constraints.

## Synthetic golden evaluation set

Use invented athlete histories with goals, events, block position, constraints, observations
and available claims. A reviewer specifies acceptable decisions, disallowed changes, supporting
claim IDs, an expected rationale and uncertainty before running the coach. Allow multiple
defensible schedules. Pin cases, corpus, rules and model versions for each evaluation.

| Synthetic case | Expected decision and rationale |
| --- | --- |
| One low recovery reading during otherwise steady training, then the same history with the reading missing | Neither input alone justifies rewriting the block. Retain the plan or ask for feedback and explain how coverage affects confidence. |
| Two applicable studies disagree, and an example program favors increasing intensity | Surface the disagreement and distinguish the program's preference. A recommendation must explain applicability and uncertainty before proposing a change. |
| A missed session would have to be doubled up during a planned taper to hit the original weekly volume | Preserve the event objective and fatigue limits. Show the deviation, give a feasible revision for approval or retain the schedule and record the missed work. |

Score factual accuracy and citation quality separately. Check each scientific statement against
the cited result and corpus version, then check whether the population, outcome and time horizon
support its use for this athlete. Count unsupported assertions, incorrect citations and omitted
material conflicts. Check the decision against allowed outcomes and the rationale against the
reviewer's criteria. Use paired cases with missing, noisy or contradictory signals to identify
unjustified changes. Check feasibility against available days, injury constraints, progression
limits, recovery spacing and the block objective. Reject a candidate release for a safety-rule
violation or an unapproved schedule change. Review regressions by case before release.

Over successive blocks, compare approved prescriptions with completed work, deviations, feedback
and performance against the block objective. Report adherence, outcome coverage and uncertainty
alongside training changes and other plausible influences. Offline case performance tests
decision quality. Longitudinal observations inform subsequent evaluation of coaching effectiveness.

Open decisions include the initial corpus scope and review cadence, the first supported block
approaches and autoregulation bounds, and quantitative acceptance thresholds for citation
accuracy and decision quality. Calibrate these on reviewed cases before the first coaching release.
