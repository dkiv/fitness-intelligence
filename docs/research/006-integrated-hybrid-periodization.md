## 1. Executive summary

The central decision is not how to maximize three separate programs. It is how to select a feasible, longitudinal joint prescription with athlete-agreed outcomes, an appropriate stimulus, sufficient recovery opportunities and nutritional support, under incomplete observation. Some qualities should improve, others may only need to be retained, and some may be consciously deprioritized. The system should preserve productive programming rather than change it merely because a week has elapsed.

**Findings that materially change the methodology:**

- There is direct evidence that organization of a concurrent program can matter even at similar assigned training dose. In a small six-week trial of well-trained ice hockey players, alternating weekly emphases improved isokinetic knee-extension torque and VO2max more than mixed weekly training. Neither the population nor the endpoints establish that alternating strength and running blocks is optimal for experienced recreational hybrid athletes. Endurance block-periodization studies likewise suggest possible benefits but broad prediction intervals include unfavorable effects. Balanced and emphasis-based approaches remain candidates.
- Reduced-dose maintenance is plausible and experimentally demonstrated for some recently acquired adaptations, but the required dose is outcome-, population- and horizon-dependent. Studies of soccer players, cyclists and recently trained adults cannot identify a universally sufficient set count for an experienced lifter. Maintaining an exposure prescription is distinct from maintaining an outcome.
- Reducing endurance frequency or duration while retaining intensity can preserve VO2max in some protocols. Long-duration endurance can nevertheless decline. Preserve running-specific exposure and assess the outcome the athlete cares about, not an interchangeable cardiovascular proxy.
- Deloading is distinct from a taper. A week of complete lifting cessation in a trained population did not clearly improve adaptations and strength estimates favored continuous training with uncertainty. A 2026 reduced-volume trial (Pancar et al.) in untrained men addresses a different intervention and different outcomes. Neither establishes the relative efficacy of scheduled versus reactive hybrid recovery weeks.
- Endurance tapering has stronger comparative evidence than integrated hybrid tapering. A review found a pooled advantage for taper versus control on time-trial performance; volume reduction with retained intensity appears reasonable, but subgroup-derived percentages do not define an individual optimum. Strength tapering guidance is based on a much smaller and heterogeneous evidence base. Do not simply combine two independent tapers.
- The most defensible first planner is hierarchical: preserve the agreed outcome priorities and block envelope while generating a detailed next week; evaluate a finite set of feasible alternatives; propose the smallest sufficient change; require approval for prescription changes. This is a candidate engineering and coaching methodology, not a proven biological control algorithm.

**Practical consequence:** select a block archetype, initialize from comparable completed exposure, specify outcome-specific secondary commitments, protect key sessions, choose provisional context-specific parameters with a recorded rationale, and distinguish execution flexibility from weekly revision and block revision. Keep strength sets, loads, running duration, intensity exposure, nutrition context and time as separate dimensions. Do not compute an unvalidated aggregate workload score or a scalar utility that pretends to predict adaptation.

**Scope of certainty:** direct concurrent evidence in truly dual-experienced runners/lifters remains sparse. No reviewed result establishes a uniquely optimal hybrid program, universal block length, maintenance fraction, deload trigger, taper protocol or marginal benefit of increasing both modalities. The synthetic programs in section 4 demonstrate a candidate decision procedure, not efficacy. Section 7 prioritizes a minimum useful J3 curation set without requiring every research question to be settled.

## 2. Scientific evidence table

### 2.1 Questions, search and appraisal conventions

Population: adults with meaningful experience in both disciplines, with indirect evidence from trained strength athletes, endurance athletes, team-sport athletes and recently trained adults as explicitly labeled. Intervention: planned or adaptive concurrent organization, reduced maintenance exposure, fatigue reduction and tapering. Comparators: alternative organization, usual training, different maintenance doses or continued training. Outcomes: maximal/exercise-specific strength, hypertrophy, power, running performance, aerobic capacity, long-duration tolerance, adherence and fatigue. Horizons: weeks to months, not a claim about lifetime adaptation.

Targeted web search on 2026-11-25: PubMed, publisher pages, accessible PMC text and indexed original-source passages. Search concepts included `concurrent block periodization strength endurance trained athletes`, `block periodization endurance meta-analysis`, `flexible daily undulating periodization trained men`, `reduced strength training dose maintenance soccer cyclists`, `Hickson reduced frequency duration intensity maintenance`, `detraining VO2max athletes`, `deload resistance training randomized reduced volume`, `endurance taper meta-analysis`, `powerlifting taper cessation`, and `deload Delphi`. Selected evidence for direct decision relevance, design, training history and identifiable results; did not restrict search to provider metrics. Prior findings are referenced rather than re-extracted. This is not a comprehensive systematic search: no registered protocol, duplicate screening, complete database exports, full study-level overlap map, or independent risk-of-bias scoring. Access labels below distinguish publisher/full-text passages, abstract-only and indexed partial text. Some PMC/publisher requests returned access errors; no inaccessible table is treated as inspected. Publication records and accessible text checked; comprehensive correction/retraction and rights checks remain outstanding. All new assertions pending; confidence labels are provisional for the specific result and generally lower for transfer to hybrid athletes.

**Synthesis.** Linear describes change across weeks; undulating describes variation between sessions or weeks; block describes concentration of emphasis; flexible describes permitted adjustment. These dimensions can coexist. H01 and H30 are the closest new direct/adjacent concurrent comparisons, but their weekly concentration and sport contexts do not test several months of alternating specialization against year-round balanced hybrid training. H02–03 partly reuse H01 data. H30 concentrates conditioning, not independently controlled resistance and running dose; crossover carryover is a concern. H04 tests constrained choice, and H06 tests a bundled adaptive running intervention, not an adaptive hybrid algorithm. The search did not identify a convincing controlled comparison of simultaneous versus sequential incremental strength and running dose in dual-experienced recreational athletes. Neither successful adherence nor an interference meta-analysis answers that marginal allocation question. A block duration must reflect event time, goal, available comparable assessment and realistic exposure, not simply the duration chosen by a study.

**Interpretation:** Retained relative loading, exercise-specific practice and tolerable exposure are sensible candidate ingredients. The minimum *gain* dose is not the maintenance dose. If an athlete currently lifts little, an arbitrary reduction may remove all meaningful exposure. If already struggling to retain an outcome, first check adherence, specificity, fatigue and fueling rather than apply a percentage. H09–13 are indirect or adjacent concurrent evidence; no universal dose floor follows.

**Joint interpretation:** deload = reduce stress to support subsequent training; taper = reduce/shape training before a specified performance date; cessation = no training in a specified discipline. They are not synonymous. Local fatigue can justify changing a lift while keeping easy running; systemic illness or widespread poor tolerance may require broader reduction or professional assessment. The search did not establish scheduled-vs-reactive superiority, a cross-discipline fatigue transfer function, or an optimal double taper. Tapering should preserve event specificity when tolerable, remove unnecessary competing work and align with the priority date. If two assessments conflict, offer an explicit tradeoff rather than promise both peaks. The Wang subgroup analysis should not be used to mandate pre-taper overload, especially with uncertain energy availability.

### 2.6 Nutrition and coordination (HP-05, HP-08) — synthesis, not new nutrition review

Use Packet 004 rather than re-extracting: `lea-combined-myofibrillar-synthesis@1` (Oxfeldt short controlled combined training), `lea-endurance-time-trial-power@1`, `energy-deficit-rt-lean-mass@1`, `energy-deficit-rt-strength@1`, `acute-cho-resistance-volume@1`, `cho-short-term-glycogen-resynthesis@1`, `rt-protein-supplement-ffm@1`, `rt-protein-ffm-breakpoint@1`, `athlete-logged-intake-dlw-disagreement@1`, `wearable-energy-expenditure-validity@1`. These support investigating energy adequacy, training-day demand, fueling of key sessions and protein provision; they do not quantify individual maintenance energy needs, prove REDs from logs or establish an optimal macro allocation for a hybrid block. A body-composition objective can conflict with performance and recovery commitments. No automatic calorie changes from estimated expenditure or a deload. Protect protein, sufficient carbohydrate for demanding sessions, dietary fat and overall energy; discuss feasibility and preferences. General guidance can be offered without observed macros. See NU-P01–08 for clinical/referral boundaries and uncertainty. Packet 007 owns estimation.

### 2.7 Measurement and proxy validity

Refer to Packet 005 for quantitative measurement agreement and repeatability, Packet 001 for strength estimates and Packet 004 for nutritional measurement. The table defines decision-specific observation requirements, not new validation findings. A measured endpoint can be informative without identifying its causal mechanism. Do not invent a smallest worthwhile change or minimum detectable change.

| Hypertrophy | Imaging or biopsy at specified sites; FFM a broader measure | Serial body mass and optional circumference/photos under comparable conditions | Mass changes reflect water, glycogen, GI content, fat and lean tissue; circumference not definitive tissue composition. No reliable hypertrophy verification from sets/body weight alone. | Qualitative prescription; effectiveness deferred without appropriate measures. |
| Power and proficiency | Jump, force-velocity or sport-specific standardized test | Optional repeated jump or technical task under comparable conditions | Maximal lift not power; task practice and fatigue matter. | Deferred for quantitative power maintenance without appropriate observations. |
| Running performance | Standardized distance time trial/race | Actual elapsed time/distance, course, conditions, effort, timing relative to load; repeated comparable tests | Weather, elevation, pacing, footwear, fatigue and motivation; Packet 005 repeatability applies only to tested protocol. | Conditional; race not always appropriate as frequent test. |
| Aerobic capacity/threshold/economy | Laboratory gas exchange, defined lactate/ventilatory protocol and standardized cost of running | Wearable estimate (if available), submax pace/HR/RPE on comparable course, sustained field performance | Not direct VO2max, threshold or economy; calibration, heat, illness, sensor error and HR lag; agreement is not longitudinal sensitivity. | Qualitative/conditional for performance tracking, deferred for direct lab constructs. |
| Durability/long-run tolerance | Change in defined physiology or performance after prolonged work | Chronological matched pace and HR segments, terrain, temperature, fueling, perceived effort and time; ability to complete comparable long runs | Aggregate means/splits without order/paired values insufficient for drift; route/sensor effects and hydration confound. | Qualitative with session feedback; quantitative decoupling deferred without eligible series. |
| Exposure and adherence | Actual completed intervention, not assigned schedule | Comparable sets/reps/load, duration/distance, purpose, optional effort, completion/deviation, known coverage; separate sports | Inferred completion and missing records cannot establish realized dose; aggregate workout steps not actual interval performance. | Conditional; structured outcomes and verified telemetry improve. |
| Fatigue / training tolerance | Task-specific performance and reported fatigue; no single direct recovery measure | Dated local soreness, effort, sleep, schedule, repeated comparable results and prior exposure; optional HRV/RHR | Stress, illness, sensor variation and local/systemic effects; single low HRV cannot identify recovery needs. | Conditional; Packet 008 to review incremental value of signals. |
| Energy adequacy | Controlled intake, expenditure, FFM and clinical assessment as appropriate | Food/weight trends with known coverage; training history, fueling and symptoms via feedback | Logs not true intake; wearable expenditure not criterion; energy balance not energy availability. Stable mass not proof of adequate fueling. | Qualitative/conditional, clinical assessment deferred/referral as appropriate. Packet 007 estimation. |

**Retention verdicts:** record the outcome, baseline method and date, follow-up method, comparability, uncertainty, and athlete-agreed practically acceptable change (not a scientific universal). Use categories: observed improvement, compatible with retention under available measurements, observed deterioration needing review, or unknown/insufficient. A nonsignificant group decline, a within-error individual change, equivalence within prespecified margins, and acceptable deterioration are different propositions. If measurement uncertainty straddles acceptable and unacceptable outcomes, state uncertainty; do not claim retention. Adherence to a maintenance exposure is separately observable.

## 3. Candidate coaching policies

### 3.1 Shared contract and revision boundaries

All policies below are **pending independent human review; operational authorization: none**. Scientific readiness concerns evidence, applicability and method review; data readiness concerns actual usable observations. Classifications refer to supportability, not approval. P/A/E = prescription, adherence and effectiveness respectively. P/A/E classifications assume the canonical input checks pass, not that the current application implements these workflows. The common requirements in this subsection are inherited by every policy.

Hard constraints include safety restrictions, available time/equipment, event-protected windows when agreed, and approved execution bounds. Objectives specify desired outcomes and relative priorities; a secondary commitment may become a hard planning constraint only when explicitly agreed and operationalized as an exposure constraint, not a guarantee of physiology. Preferences guide selection between feasible alternatives. Provisional parameters (e.g., set count, progression increment, training window) are reviewer-selected, contextual, versioned and revisable; none is a universal safety boundary. Conflicting hard constraints produce infeasibility rather than silent relaxation. New pain, illness or concerning nutritional/health patterns trigger appropriate protection, clarification or referral; do not optimize through a restriction. The athlete may always stop a session. A coach cannot diagnose the underlying cause or promise safety from a metric.

Every proposed prescription change must pass feasibility checks and receive explicit athlete approval; an execution choice inside an already approved range does not change the prescription. Read-only interpretation never writes a plan. Preserve old prescription, context, observations, evidence/rule versions, rationale, execution links and decisions. A new restriction makes a stale proposal ineligible for approval. Do not treat missing observations as zero, infer completion from an activity count, or impute a physiological construct as measured. Ask for feedback only when it materially improves a decision; do not mandate all optional tracking. Keep quantitative adaptation separate from qualitative guidance and use an independently supportable fallback where available. For actual efficacy, prospective comparison to simpler coaching baselines remains needed.

### HP-P04 revision 0.1 — Strength maintenance during endurance emphasis

- **Decision / constructs:** choose movement-specific resistance stimulus preserving strength, skill and, if explicitly requested, muscle mass/power while freeing endurance resources.

**Proposal selection without an invented utility.** First eliminate hard-constraint violations. Then classify each feasible option by (a) which objective it advances, (b) which exposure/secondary commitment it preserves or changes, (c) comparable evidence and uncertainty, (d) time/tolerance/fueling requirements, and (e) how disruptive it is. Do not claim Pareto optimality of predicted physiology; compare known commitments and explicitly labeled judgments. If one option meets the same commitments without an unnecessary change, it is a reasonable conservative candidate. If alternatives genuinely trade off objectives, present them for preference confirmation. Stable tie-breaking between equivalent calendar options may use agreed preference and deterministic ordering, not a physiological superiority claim.

A qualified reviewer selects numerical parameters by anchoring them to actual recent comparable dose and available time, choosing a tested or customary *candidate* appropriate to the goal, documenting transfer limits and the smallest practical adjustment, selecting a plausible alternative, and checking the candidate in synthetic and prospective evaluation. Record units, baseline, allowable endpoints, phase, contraindications, approval and rule version. Do not turn study parameters into universal limits. If necessary inputs are unavailable, generate a lower-specificity plan only if independently defensible, otherwise defer. Athlete consent does not itself validate a scientific premise.

### 4.3 Adaptation alternatives and review loop

| Observed pattern | Candidate action(s) | Why / what not to infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both outcomes improving and burden acceptable | Continue; optionally progress one or both within reviewed bounds if useful for goal | Completion is not proof further dose helps. |
| Primary improves, secondary plausibly declines | Check comparability, adherence and current exposure; restore or redistribute secondary work; offer revised tradeoff | Not automatic failure or proof of interference. |
| Stable comparable performance, acceptable burden | Hold; if development goal and credible plateau persists, consider specificity/dose change | Do not infer plateau from sparse observations. |
| Poor adherence | Reduce logistical friction, move or simplify before increasing exposure | Assigned dose not actual stimulus. |
| Local fatigue | Modify affected movement/run demand, leave unrelated work if feasible | Local soreness not universal systemic fatigue. |
| General fatigue or declining both outcomes | Check illness, sleep, stress, fueling and exposure; hold escalation, reduce burden or refer as appropriate | No single signal determines cause. |
| Schedule/event change | Reallocate within envelope, or revise commitments with consent | Do not silently drop an objective. |
| Sparse/noisy/conflicting data | Ask targeted feedback, preserve safe productive work, defer quantitative change | Missing data not poor response or recovery. |

### 4.6 Illustrative programs — common rules and definitions

All numbers in examples A–C below, including baselines, targets, effort ranges, sets, durations, progressions, time windows, phases and review tolerances, are **synthetic/provisional (P)** fixtures, not reviewed recommendations. They intentionally show decisions rather than optimize a real athlete. They assume healthy adult athletes with no current contraindication, familiar movements and prior tolerated running/strength work. All working sets exclude warm-ups. RPE on a 0–10 perceived exertion scale; lifting RPE is set-specific, running RPE is effort/purpose, not the same construct. Percentage loads refer to the explicitly pinned synthetic measured 1RM, not a changing e1RM. Use familiar safe equipment, warm-ups and appropriate rest; stop for concerning symptoms. Each schedule includes warm-up, transitions and recovery within stated time budget; if time becomes inadequate, trim low-priority accessory work only within approved bounds or request review. Time-based runs include warm-up/cool-down as specified. No prescribed pace is inferred from a wearable zone label. If planned effort and pace conflict, use the approved easier effort option or record deviation. A later week is an illustrative alternative trajectory, never automatically activated.

For all examples, nutrition guidance is conditional on appetite, preferences, actual training and health context: regular meals with adequate energy and protein distributed across meals, carbohydrate-rich familiar food before key sessions and after as needed, hydration according to conditions and individual tolerance. Long/race sessions may warrant practiced carbohydrate during exercise consistent with Packet 004 guidance (e.g., 30–60 g/h as a consensus reference for endurance work of roughly 1–2.5 h, not a required intake or synthetic calorie target). Start with familiar tolerated fueling; short easy sessions do not automatically require supplements. Do not prescribe exact calories without context or reduce food automatically on recovery days. All example outcomes/thresholds are agreed synthetic fixture values, not minimum detectable change estimates.

## 8. Human review checklist

- [ ] H01 obtain original report; verify randomization, sex/training status, actual strength/endurance modality and dose matching, dispersion and direct contrasts; distinguish torque from maximal lift and VO2max from running performance. H02/H03 inspect included study list and hockey overlap, prediction intervals and low study quality; do not count overlapping trials twice.
- [ ] H30/H31 inspect full crossover analysis, period/sequence/carryover, realized conditioning exposure and unmonitored lifting, multiplicity, exact d definition and CI. - [ ] H09/H10 verify Bickel baseline experience, exact training dose, age-specific muscle measures, attrition and outcome definitions; H11 confirm original Table 1, 3×4RM and direct contrasts; H12 verify preceding development vs maintenance, background endurance and interval estimates.
